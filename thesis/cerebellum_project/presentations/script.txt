**Slide 5: The challenges to Ito's control theory**
"However, empirical data challenged this. Work by Pasalar and colleagues showed that when subjects track targets through different force fields, Purkinje cells don't actually encode 'required forces'. They seem to be encoding something else entirely."

### **Part 2: The Shift to Forward Models (Slides 6–10)**

**Slide 6: The challenges to Ito's control theory (Encoding)**
"If it isn't force, what is it? P-cells encode state space, such as position (\theta), with high reliability (R^2 > 0.5), but adding force parameters doesn't improve the model. This suggests the cerebellum is not outputting a motor command, but a prediction of state."

**Slide 7: The challenges to Ito's control theory (Herzfeld)**
"Recent work by Herzfeld has refined this further. We now know that P-cells are forward models encoding the sensory consequences of movement. Furthermore, the climbing fiber is no longer viewed as a simple 'on/off' error switch; it is a complex signal tuple encoding both the direction (spatial) and magnitude (timing) of the error."

**Slide 8: The nail in the coffin (?)**
"Finally, Diedrichsen and King's recent parcellations show that the cerebellum is highly specialized, with regions seemingly dedicated to specific tasks that don't follow anatomical lobules. This led some to believe the Universal Transform might be a myth."

**Slide 9: My rebuttal to King, Diedrichsen, and others**
"My rebuttal is that we are falling into a 'projector trap.' We are mistaking the input data for the machine's function. If a machine corrects motor data (A \to A') and social data (B \to B'), the outputs look different, but the transformation T could be identical. We need a language to describe this invariance: Category Theory."

**Slide 10: Granule expansion permits a universal construct**
"The granule layer is our 'Universal Translator.' It lifts multimodal inputs into a massive, sparse Hilbert Space. Whether the input is a joint angle or a social cue, the expansion strips the semantics, leaving only a geometric pattern for the Purkinje cell to process. Herzfeld's error rule then updates the weights based on this context."
